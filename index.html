WHAT A LESSON MIGHT LOOK LIKE

Start with what it needs to enable.

Take something as fundamental as your grip. Rather than beginning with a textbook picture, we might start with a question:

What does your grip need to enable you to do?

Change something. Try it. What changed? There is still a purpose, and I’m still coaching. But we’ve started with function, not an ideal picture.

This is the kind of exploration a lesson might involve: a clear question, a purposeful task and enough freedom to discover what changes.

QUESTIONS

A few things you might be curious about.

How does the Meet Me conversation take place?

The free 30-minute conversation can be in person, by video call or by telephone—whichever feels most convenient for you. We’ll talk about your golf, what you’re curious about and whether Parker Golf feels like the right fit. No equipment is needed.

Will you tell me what I’m doing wrong?

I’ll help you understand what is happening and what may be worth changing. Sometimes the most useful guidance is technical information; sometimes it’s a question, task or changed constraint that lets you feel the difference for yourself.

Do you teach the golf swing?

Yes. Technical coaching is part of the work when it helps. I’ll explain relevant principles and give you clear information, while using Guided Discovery to help you explore how those ideas work in your own movement and ball flight.

Is this just trial and error?

No. It’s exploration with direction. Each task, question, constraint and piece of information has a purpose, so you can learn from the feedback rather than simply guess at solutions.

Is Guided Discovery suitable for beginners?

Absolutely. You don’t need to arrive with technical knowledge or a particular swing. We’ll start with what you want to do, understand or experience, and build from there.

Do I need clubs or other equipment?

No equipment is needed for the free Meet Me conversation—just a little curiosity. If equipment is relevant to what you want to explore, we can discuss what you use, what might be useful and how it fits into your learning. For a first lesson, bring whatever you already have available; we’ll decide together what matters.

What if I prefer technical advice?

That’s welcome. The aim isn’t to avoid technical detail—it’s to use the right amount at the right time, then connect it to something you can feel, test and apply.
